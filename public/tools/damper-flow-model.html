How the numbers are made

      

        Face velocity is CFM ÷ (W×H/144) and velocity pressure is (V/4005)² at standard air.
        Full-open pressure drop is Cd × Pv with Cd per profile.
      

      

        Blade angle enters through free area. Blades are pitched to fill the height, so with n blades the
        projected blockage is n·(b·cosθ + t·sinθ), giving free-area fraction
        α(θ) = 1 − cosθ − (n·t/H)·sinθ — zero at closed, 1 − n·t/H at full open.
        Throttling loss then scales as an orifice: C(θ) = Cpublished + Cblade·((αopen/α)ⁿ − 1),
        which returns exactly TAMCO's published number at full open and grows as the gap closes.
      

      

        The K-factor falls out of that directly: CFM = K√ΔP with K = 4005·A ÷ √C(θ), A in ft² and ΔP in in. w.g.
        Leakage is derived rather than fitted — every AMCA class in TAMCO's table is precisely
        base × √ΔP cfm/ft² (base = 3, 4, 10, 40 for classes 1A, 1, 2, 3), which reproduces all thirteen published numeric
        cells to TAMCO's own rounding. Class 1A is rated at 1 in. w.g. only, so its other three cells read n/a.
      

    

    

      
What to distrust

      

        The WP number is not the damper. TAMCO's WP table is a plenum test: its "Damper & System" column is dominated by
        the opening's own entry loss. Its "Damper Only" column is negative in all thirty published rows — the air-foil blades
        cost less than the bare hole. Read the WP pressure drop as the opening plus the damper, never as the damper alone.
      

      

        SP and NP full-open coefficients were read off a chart, and they depend on the size. TAMCO tabulates nothing for
        those profiles — only the AMCA Fig. 5.3 log-log curves, and that figure is five separate curves whose implied
        coefficients span roughly 0.18 to 0.70 (SP) and 0.30 to 1.04 (NP). A single blanket coefficient — the usual shortcut —
        is about 2× wrong in the middle of that range. This page resolves to the tested size nearest your opening and says which
        one it used. Treat every one of them as planning-grade: they were read by eye, and the lowest curves are the least certain.
      

      

        Everything about partly-closed blades is a model. TAMCO certifies full-open performance only. A multi-blade damper's
        downstream field is genuinely messy — jets between blades, wakes off each edge, and for parallel action a flow that biases to
        one side — which is exactly why no manufacturer publishes a universal partial-open K for rectangular dampers. For a
        measuring application the model picks your aperture; a field traverse sets your K.
      

      

        Near closed, this model is wrong on purpose. The orifice term runs away toward infinity while the real damper simply
        leaks at its AMCA class. The page solves for the angle at which the modelled path passes less than the seals leak, names it,
        and marks everything below it as not physical — rather than pretending the curve is still valid.
      

      

        Blade action is drawn, not calculated. Opposed and parallel return identical numbers here, because TAMCO publishes no
        separate data for them. The difference is real — parallel throws the discharge to one side — but nobody has quantified it for
        this damper, so the page gives you the advice and declines to invent the arithmetic.
